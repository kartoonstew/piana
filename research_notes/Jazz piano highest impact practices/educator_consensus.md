# Educator Consensus on Highest-Leverage Practices for New Jazz Improvisers / Jazz Pianists

Research date: 2026-11-02. Scope: what named jazz educators and canonical texts say gives the most progress per hour, where they agree and disagree, sequencing, first repertoire, and traps. Target reader: an advanced classically trained pianist new to jazz and blues.

Source-quality note: Most book content (Levine, Crook, Coker, Ligon, DeGreg) is only reachable online as publisher blurbs, tables of contents, and reviews. Primary-voice material was found for Aebersold (2018 interview), Ethan Iverson (blog, ~Dec 2018), Hal Galper (his own site), Kenny Werner (Berklee article), and Wynton Marsalis (his foundation site). Barry Harris material is mostly secondhand: profiles, obituaries, research summaries of workshop transcriptions, and forums. Two fetches failed (Frédéric Galliano's Barry Harris notes at irfu.cea.fr refused the connection; the jazzguitar.be Barry Harris practice thread returned 403). Anything not verified this session is in the Gaps sections, labeled "to verify."

## Q1. What do individual educators say a beginner-to-intermediate player should spend time on?

### Takeaway
Across very different camps, nearly every educator with a primary-source statement ranks **listening to and imitating recordings** as the foundation. They then split on the "engine" for building vocabulary: chord-scale theory (Aebersold, Coker, Haerle lineage), chord-tone/bebop-language systems (Barry Harris, Ligon, Galper's target notes), or psychological/attentional work (Werner). For pianists specifically, the canonical texts (Levine, DeGreg) put **voicing fluency, made automatic in all keys,** at the center of the left-hand/comping side.

### Cited Findings

**Jamey Aebersold (play-along series, from 1967)**
- Aebersold says students need the basics first: "If the chord is a C chord, that is the basic scale students should play off of." He found many students didn't know their scales and chords, which left them lost even on the blues. — [The Instrumentalist, "After 50 Years of Play-A-Longs" (Jan 2018)](https://theinstrumentalist.com/january-2018/after-50-years-of-play-a-longs-perspectives-on-jazz-education/)
- His core materials: scales, chords, melodies such as "Perdido" and "Satin Doll," and "numerous blues in Bb and F." — [The Instrumentalist (2018)](https://theinstrumentalist.com/january-2018/after-50-years-of-play-a-longs-perspectives-on-jazz-education/)
- "People who want to learn jazz have to listen to jazz." He recommends small combos over big bands for listening, because big-band soloing is limited and the backgrounds are busy. — [The Instrumentalist (2018)](https://theinstrumentalist.com/january-2018/after-50-years-of-play-a-longs-perspectives-on-jazz-education/)
- Ear training "has to be coupled with application," meaning knowing how roots, 3rds, 5ths and 7ths relate to the harmony. — [The Instrumentalist (2018)](https://theinstrumentalist.com/january-2018/after-50-years-of-play-a-longs-perspectives-on-jazz-education/)
- Singing: "anyone can improvise." People can often sing a convincing solo over a simple progression before they can play one. "It's the instrument that holds us back." — [The Instrumentalist (2018)](https://theinstrumentalist.com/january-2018/after-50-years-of-play-a-longs-perspectives-on-jazz-education/)
- Beginners can start by improvising over a single scale to build confidence. His own first teaching was having students play over a Dorian scale. — [The Instrumentalist (2018)](https://theinstrumentalist.com/january-2018/after-50-years-of-play-a-longs-perspectives-on-jazz-education/); [Thibeault, JRME 2021 (abstract)](https://repository.eduhk.hk/en/publications/aebersolds-mediated-play-a-long-pedagogy-and-the-invention-of-the/)
- He criticizes players who "play seemingly randomly" over the harmony. — [The Instrumentalist (2018)](https://theinstrumentalist.com/january-2018/after-50-years-of-play-a-longs-perspectives-on-jazz-education/)
- Chord-scale theory comes from George Russell's Lydian Chromatic Concept and was popularized by Aebersold, David Baker, and Jerry Coker. — [Open Music Theory (Univ. of Nebraska Pressbooks)](https://pressbooks.nebraska.edu/openmusictheory/?p=504)

**Barry Harris (Jazz Workshops; d. 2021)**
- Harris learned bebop largely by ear from records, starting with Bud Powell on "Webb City" (with Sonny Stitt and Fats Navarro), and used that to develop his soloing. — [Wikipedia: Barry Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Harris)
- A central message of his materials is that anyone can learn to play the music. A student quoted in a profile says he taught that you need to learn the rules so you can bend them. He reportedly disliked musicians who "just like sitting around playing patterns." — [Tune Into Leadership profile](https://www.tuneintoleadership.com/newsletter/barry-harris-keeper-of-the-bebop-flame); [All About Jazz profile](https://www.allaboutjazz.com/barry-harris-a-thousand-percent-music-man-barry-harris-by-andrew-velez.php) (secondhand; the exact source of the "patterns" quote isn't pinned down)
- His weekly NYC workshops mixed students aged 20 to 60 and of widely varying ability. — [All About Jazz](https://www.allaboutjazz.com/barry-harris-a-thousand-percent-music-man-barry-harris-by-andrew-velez.php)
- His official tutorial covers the major and minor 6th-diminished scales and "basic sixth and diminished chord movement." — [barryharris.com tutorial](https://barryharris.com/music-improvisation-tutorial)
- A research project built on transcriptions of his workshops puts the diminished chord at the center: chords relate to the diminished, and the diminished is used to connect chords and create "movement." — [Vera Marijt, Research Catalogue](https://www.researchcatalogue.net/view/31695/31696)
- Practice suggestion for the 6th-diminished scale: play it up, down, and in random order, then in 3rds, then harmonize it as chords. For tonic minor, use the minor-6th-diminished scale. — [FreeJazzLessons "Barry Harris Harmony Hacks"](https://www.freejazzlessons.com/?p=13635); [Galliano notes (search snippet; fetch failed)](https://irfu.cea.fr/Pisp/frederic.galliano/Zique/barry_harris.html)
- Forum characterization: Harris, Hal Galper, and Ethan Iverson share a critique of "scale based beginner pedagogy." A counter-poster notes Harris "details a whole bunch of strategies to go from scales to lines." — [jazzguitar.be forum](https://www.jazzguitar.be/forum/theory/73218-chord-scale-theory-critique-not-mine-8.html) (forum opinion, not a Harris quote)
- A forum anecdote says Harris told Berklee students in a masterclass that they were "teaching it all wrong." Another poster disputed it, and neither gave a source. — [jazzguitar.be Barry Harris thread](https://www.jazzguitar.be/forum/improvisation/55582-official-barry-harris-thread-39.html) (hearsay)

**Hal Galper (*Forward Motion: From Bach to Bebop*)**
- Galper's core claim is that "one" is a resolution beat: "'One' is a resolution beat, a point of rest for the ear and stops the line." Lines should be heard as moving toward 1 (and 3) from tension beats such as the "and" of 2, rather than starting on 1. — [halgalper.com, "Understanding Forward Motion"](https://halgalper.com/articles/understandingforwardmotion/)
- He opens with Dizzy Gillespie: "The more upbeats you have in the music the more it swings." — [halgalper.com](https://halgalper.com/articles/understandingforwardmotion/)
- Exercises: play the target notes (chord tones and guide tones on beats 1 and 3) first, then approach each one from a scale tone above, then add pickups to scales, arpeggios, and intervals. — [JazzGuitarLessons.net summary](https://learn.jazzguitarlessons.net/blog/forward-motion); [halgalper.com](https://halgalper.com/articles/understandingforwardmotion/)
- He calls mechanical repetition an outmoded way to practice and says music is "almost universally taught" backwards. Scales should be converted from key scales into chord scales as they function over changes. "To change the way you play you have to change the way you think." His focus is retraining perception and hearing ahead ("two beats, four beats, two bars, eight bars ahead"). — [halgalper.com](https://halgalper.com/articles/understandingforwardmotion/)
- The book grew out of his Down Beat articles of 1980–81, which emphasized "playing the strong beats of the chord on the strong beats of the bar." — [Goodreads / book description](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/1372844.Forward_Motion); [Local 802 review](https://www.local802afm.org/allegro/articles/802-bookshelf-forward-motion-from-bach-to-bebop-a-corrective-approach-to-jazz-playing/)

**Kenny Werner (*Effortless Mastery*, 1996)**
- The four steps: (1) touch the instrument without thought, from "the Space"; (2) move around the instrument without thought or judgment; (3) play what you already know without judging yourself; (4) study from the Space, without pressure toward a goal. — [Berklee, "Reconnecting with Your Instrument"](https://www.berklee.edu/node/2474676)
- "When we try to play better, we play worse. When we relax and forgive ourselves, we play better." He names "Music School Disease" and tying self-worth to performance as damaging. — [Berklee](https://www.berklee.edu/node/2474676)
- On material: Werner draws on Bill Evans's "I practice the minimum," meaning less material, not less time. He argues you implement more of what you practice when you cover less, and warns that the ego pushes you to cover more material in less time. — [Guitar Practices Substack, Effortless Mastery Pt. 3](https://guitarpractices.substack.com/p/effortless-mastery-part-3-doing-the) (secondary summary)

**Bert Ligon (*Connecting Chords with Linear Harmony*, Hal Leonard 1996)**
- The book studies three melodic "outlines" (skeletons) drawn from hundreds of examples by great jazz players. It starts from basic 3rd-to-7th resolutions between chords and covers common embellishments, with exercises to get the outlines "under your fingers." — [JazzGuitarLessons.net](https://learn.jazzguitarlessons.net/blog/connecting-chords-with-linear-harmony); [eJazzLines](https://ejazzlines.com/connecting-chords-with-linear-harmony-bert-ligon)
- The outlines are also Chapter 15 of Ligon's *Comprehensive Technique for Jazz Musicians*, in less depth. — [jazzguitar.be forum](https://www.jazzguitar.be/forum/improvisation/29618-bert-ligon-connecting-comprehensive-book-difference.html)

**Mark Levine (*The Jazz Piano Book*, 1989)**
- The book covers left-hand voicings, scales and modes, improvisation, chords, and comping. The left-hand voicing chapter teaches two positions, "A" and "B," with the left hand carrying the whole chord so the right hand is free. Voicings should be chosen to fit the melody note. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jazz_Piano_Book)
- Levine treats left-hand and two-handed voicings as equally valid (whole tunes can be played with left-hand voicings) but emphasizes two-handed voicings for color and fullness. Chapters include altering notes in left-hand voicings, stride, Bud Powell voicings, block chords, and comping. — [Coleman's Music listing](https://www.colemansmusic.com.au/products/the-jazz-piano-book-mark-levine); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jazz_Piano_Book)

**Phil DeGreg (*Jazz Keyboard Harmony*, Aebersold/Alfred)**
- The book's goal is to make voicings "automatic and intuitive." It moves step by step from 2-voice to 7-voice structures, including 4ths and upper-structure triads, with each voicing type shown in essential progressions in all keys with fingerings. Play-along audio supports drilling the exercises and comping behind a soloist. Endorsed by Levine; the author's site claims more than 20,000 copies sold. — [phildegreg.com/book](https://phildegreg.com/book/); [eJazzLines](https://ejazzlines.com/jazz-keyboard-harmony-phil-degreg)

**Hal Crook (*How to Improvise*) and Jerry Coker (*Improvising Jazz*)**
- Crook: a 188-page method with audio that addresses rhythm, melody, and harmony and offers "a practice routine for developing improvisational technique." — [Boosey listing](https://boosey.com/shop/prod/Crook-Hal-How-To-Improvise/2142833); [Hebu](https://hebu-music.com/en/article/hal-crook/advance-music-veronika-gruber-gmbh/how-to-improvise.290110)
- Coker: a self-teaching guide that uses fundamental tools (melody, rhythm, superimposed chords) to build a personal melodic style. — [Simon & Schuster](https://www.simonandschuster.ca/books/Improvising-Jazz/Jerry-Coker/9780671628291)

**Peter Martin / Adam Maness (Open Studio; *You'll Hear It* podcast)**
- "7 Ways to Learn Jazz Faster" (ep. dated Nov 12, 2019 on the page): Listen; Practice; Be smart about your practice; Stay inspired; Get a teacher; [Open Studio courses]; Check out things outside jazz. Listening is #1. — [You'll Hear It](https://youllhearit.com/episodes/7-ways-to-learn-jazz-faster)
- "7 Highly Effective Habits": Listen, Daily practice, Practice patience, Practice journal, Transcribe, Scales, Compose. — [You'll Hear It](https://youllhearit.transistor.fm/episodes/7-highly-effective-habits-to-make-you-a-better-jazz-musician) (title and list from search snippet)
- Open Studio's "Jazz Piano for Beginners" course targets players with basic instrumental control who are new to jazz ("aspiring jazz pianist to functional jazz pianist"). Practice sessions are led by Adam Maness. Martin also sells "Essential Jazz Piano Etudes," 27 technique etudes for daily practice. — [Open Studio](https://www.openstudiojazz.com/?p=57776); [Open Studio Etudes](https://www.openstudiojazz.com/?p=520955)
- Other episode topics relevant to efficiency: 15-minute practice sessions, practicing away from the instrument, practicing in all keys, "4 Chords to Rule Them All," and "Don't Do These 7 Things When You Practice." — [You'll Hear It episode index](https://youllhearit.transistor.fm/episodes/how-to-crush-a-15-minute-practice-session)

**Ethan Iverson (pianist, The Bad Plus; blog *Do the M@th*), ~Dec 2018**
- Chord-scale teaching is "received wisdom," which he defines as "common knowledge that is held to be true, but may not be." Teachers "teach not what the master does, but shortcuts to a simulacrum." — [Iverson, "Received Wisdom"](https://ethaniverson.com/received-wisdom-jeff-goldblum-chord-scales-the-ireal-book-and-kamasi-washington/)
- "Blues licks do not fit a chord scale." Bebop masters rarely use Lydian on the tonic of "Out of Nowhere." "After all these years of jazz education, the sound of chord scales disconnected from bebop and the blues has inexorably crept into the fabric of common-practice jazz." — [Iverson](https://ethaniverson.com/received-wisdom-jeff-goldblum-chord-scales-the-ireal-book-and-kamasi-washington/)
- The iReal Book offers "a banal table of chords" and "leaves out the most relevant pieces of information a jazz master uses." "No master ever played any changes of any song without consulting the melody first." His remedy: "keep checking out the source material, not the textbooks," and keep blues, the Songbook, and Monk central. — [Iverson](https://ethaniverson.com/received-wisdom-jeff-goldblum-chord-scales-the-ireal-book-and-kamasi-washington/)

**Famous-player advice**
- **Bill Evans** (*The Universal Mind of Bill Evans*, 1966 film): "It's better to do something simple which is real… Whereas, if you try to approximate something very advanced and you don't know what you're doing, you can't build on it." He criticized approaching a problem "in its entirety in a vague, abstract way." On impatience: "Most people just don't realize the immensity of the problem… either because they can't conquer immediately they think they haven't got the ability, or they're so impatient to conquer it that they never do see it through." Freedom "only is free insofar as it has reference to the strictness of the original form." — [Presentation Zen](https://presentationzen.com/blog/lessons-from-bill-evans); [Open Culture](https://www.openculture.com/page/1129) (quotes come from secondary write-ups of the film, not a verified transcript)
- **Clark Terry**: "Imitation, Assimilation, Innovation." JazzAdvice's application: learn lines by ear and transcribe (absorbing feel, articulation, and time), repeat each line until automatic, take it through all 12 keys and registers, start slowly, sing the rhythms and pitches, and only then innovate. Many students try to skip straight to innovation. — [JazzAdvice](https://www.jazzadvice.com/lessons/clark-terrys-3-steps-to-learning-improvisation/). The autobiography version reportedly reads "Emulate, assimilate, innovate." — [Earl MacDonald on Terry's autobiography](https://earlmacdonald.com/clark-terry-autobiography)
- **Wynton Marsalis** "12 Ways to Practice" (1996): get instruction; keep a schedule; set realistic goals; concentrate ("You can do more in 10 minutes of focused practice than in an hour of sighing and moaning"); relax and practice slowly; practice what you can't play; play with maximum expression; don't be too hard on yourself; don't show off; think for yourself ("don't rely on methods"); be optimistic; look for connections to other things. — [Wynton Marsalis Foundation](https://wyntonmarsalis.org/news/entry/wynton-marsalis-12-tips-on-how-to-practice-for-musicians-athletes-anyone); [Open Culture](https://www.openculture.com/2017/04/wynton-marsalis-gives-12-tips-on-how-to-practice-for-musicians-athletes-or-anyone-who-wants-to-learn-something-new.html)

### Inferences
- Primary-source agreement is strongest on **listening/imitating recordings** (Aebersold, Harris's own learning path, Martin's #1, Terry, Iverson). This holds even for Aebersold, the figure most associated with the scale approach.
- The pianist-specific texts (Levine, DeGreg) define the left-hand problem as **voicing vocabulary made automatic across keys, chosen against the melody note**. This is a technical-drill problem that a classically trained pianist can likely move through much faster than an average beginner.
- Galper's and Ligon's systems converge on the same practical move: **guide-tone/target-note lines landing chord tones on strong beats**. Ligon arrives at it via 3rd-to-7th resolutions and outlines, Galper via rhythmic placement. Harris's chord-tone/6th-diminished thinking is consistent with this, though his voice is mostly secondhand here.
- Werner and Evans (via Werner's "practice the minimum") add a meta-principle: **narrow the material and go deep**. That counters the classically trained player's habit of covering repertoire broadly.

## Q2. Where is there strong consensus, and where are points contested?

### Takeaway
Consensus: listen constantly; learn by ear and imitate (transcribe at least fragments); learn tunes/melodies, not just changes; build chord-tone/guide-tone awareness; internalize swing and time; go deep rather than wide. Contested: chord-scale theory vs. bebop-language/chord-tone systems (the biggest fault line), the value of Aebersold/iReal play-alongs, and how much theory to front-load.

### Cited Findings

**Consensus items (multiple independent sources)**
- *Listen to records, a lot.* Aebersold: "People who want to learn jazz have to listen to jazz." — [The Instrumentalist](https://theinstrumentalist.com/january-2018/after-50-years-of-play-a-longs-perspectives-on-jazz-education/). Martin ranks "Listen" first. — [You'll Hear It](https://youllhearit.com/episodes/7-ways-to-learn-jazz-faster). Iverson: "keep checking out the source material." — [Iverson](https://ethaniverson.com/received-wisdom-jeff-goldblum-chord-scales-the-ireal-book-and-kamasi-washington/)
- *Imitate/transcribe by ear.* Terry's imitate stage — [JazzAdvice](https://www.jazzadvice.com/lessons/clark-terrys-3-steps-to-learning-improvisation/). Harris learned from records by ear — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Harris). "Transcribe" is one of Martin's 7 habits — [You'll Hear It](https://youllhearit.transistor.fm/episodes/7-highly-effective-habits-to-make-you-a-better-jazz-musician). Learn Jazz Standards recommends picking apart the Miles ("All Blues") and Rollins ("St. Thomas") solos — [LJS](https://www.learnjazzstandards.com/blog/20-easy-jazz-standards/)
- *Sing.* Aebersold says people can sing a convincing solo before they can play one — [The Instrumentalist](https://theinstrumentalist.com/january-2018/after-50-years-of-play-a-longs-perspectives-on-jazz-education/). JazzAdvice/Terry: sing the rhythms, accents, and exact pitches — [JazzAdvice](https://www.jazzadvice.com/lessons/clark-terrys-3-steps-to-learning-improvisation/)
- *Melody first; tunes as the unit of study.* Iverson: "No master ever played any changes of any song without consulting the melody first." — [Iverson](https://ethaniverson.com/received-wisdom-jeff-goldblum-chord-scales-the-ireal-book-and-kamasi-washington/). Aebersold's basic material includes melodies like "Perdido" and "Satin Doll." — [The Instrumentalist](https://theinstrumentalist.com/january-2018/after-50-years-of-play-a-longs-perspectives-on-jazz-education/). LJS: standards "contain the chords, phrases, and progressions needed to become fluent." — [LJS](https://www.learnjazzstandards.com/blog/20-easy-jazz-standards/)
- *Chord tones / guide tones on strong beats.* Galper's target notes on 1 and 3 — [halgalper.com](https://halgalper.com/articles/understandingforwardmotion/). Ligon's 3rd-to-7th resolutions — [JazzGuitarLessons.net](https://learn.jazzguitarlessons.net/blog/connecting-chords-with-linear-harmony). Aebersold: ear training should relate roots, 3rds, 5ths, and 7ths to the harmony — [The Instrumentalist](https://theinstrumentalist.com/january-2018/after-50-years-of-play-a-longs-perspectives-on-jazz-education/)
- *All 12 keys.* JazzAdvice/Terry — [JazzAdvice](https://www.jazzadvice.com/lessons/clark-terrys-3-steps-to-learning-improvisation/); DeGreg's voicings in all keys — [phildegreg.com](https://phildegreg.com/book/); Martin's episode "How to Practice in All Keys" — [You'll Hear It](https://youllhearit.transistor.fm/episodes/how-to-practice-in-all-keys)
- *Depth over breadth, slow and focused.* Evans — [Presentation Zen](https://presentationzen.com/blog/lessons-from-bill-evans); Werner — [Substack summary](https://guitarpractices.substack.com/p/effortless-mastery-part-3-doing-the); Marsalis ("10 minutes of focused practice," "practice slowly," "practice what you can't play") — [Wynton Marsalis Foundation](https://wyntonmarsalis.org/news/entry/wynton-marsalis-12-tips-on-how-to-practice-for-musicians-athletes-anyone)
- *Rhythm and upbeats drive swing.* Galper, quoting Gillespie: "The more upbeats you have in the music the more it swings." — [halgalper.com](https://halgalper.com/articles/understandingforwardmotion/)

**Contested points**
- *Chord-scale theory vs. bebop/blues language.*
  - Pro-scale: Aebersold ("If the chord is a C chord, that is the basic scale students should play off of"). — [The Instrumentalist](https://theinstrumentalist.com/january-2018/after-50-years-of-play-a-longs-perspectives-on-jazz-education/)
  - Against: Iverson calls chord scales "received wisdom" and "shortcuts to a simulacrum." — [Iverson](https://ethaniverson.com/received-wisdom-jeff-goldblum-chord-scales-the-ireal-book-and-kamasi-washington/)
  - Galper: scales are taught as written rather than as used, and teaching is "backwards." — [halgalper.com](https://halgalper.com/articles/understandingforwardmotion/)
  - Academic: Keith Salley argues chord-scale teaching fails to prepare improvisers in two ways and proposes a species-counterpoint approach. — [Salley, JMTP, "Beyond Chord-Scale Theory"](https://journals-upgrade.shareok.org/jmtp/article/view/677). A Univ. of Miami doctoral essay says its bebop/modal roots limit it on tonally vague post-bop changes. — [U. Miami](https://scholarship.miami.edu/esploro/outputs/doctoral/Linear-Chord-Scale-Analysis-and-Improvisation/991032013404702976)
  - Middle position: Ed Byrne argues only that chord-scale theory "is not the best starting point." — [jazzguitar.be forum citing Byrne](https://www.jazzguitar.be/forum/theory/73218-chord-scale-theory-critique-not-mine-8.html)
  - Historical defense: Thibeault (JRME 2021) frames the chord-scale approach as Black music theory (via George Russell) and reviews criticisms of the Aebersold model. — [Thibeault abstract](https://repository.eduhk.hk/en/publications/aebersolds-mediated-play-a-long-pedagogy-and-the-invention-of-the/)
  - Iverson concedes scale theory "is not hard for somebody who already knows how to play jazz" and is valid for specific effects, as in Coltrane, Tyner, and Henderson. — [Iverson](https://ethaniverson.com/received-wisdom-jeff-goldblum-chord-scales-the-ireal-book-and-kamasi-washington/)
- *Play-alongs (Aebersold / iReal).*
  - Pro: Aebersold says his pedagogical play-alongs changed how musicians practice. — [The Instrumentalist](https://theinstrumentalist.com/january-2018/after-50-years-of-play-a-longs-perspectives-on-jazz-education/). DeGreg builds play-along audio into voicing drills. — [phildegreg.com](https://phildegreg.com/book/)
  - Con: Iverson says the iReal Book is "a repository of received wisdom" with "a banal table of chords." — [Iverson](https://ethaniverson.com/received-wisdom-jeff-goldblum-chord-scales-the-ireal-book-and-kamasi-washington/). A forum critic says the series teaches only chord/scale, "not the older method of basing improv off of the chord arpeggios of the song." — [Steel Guitar Forum](https://be.steelguitarforum.com/viewtopic.php?p=2672341) (low-authority)
- *Theory first vs. ear first.* Harris reportedly taught "learn the rules so you can bend them." — [Tune Into Leadership](https://www.tuneintoleadership.com/newsletter/barry-harris-keeper-of-the-bebop-flame). Forum practitioners split: some say scales and arpeggios make learning by ear easier, others say many experienced players recommend ear first and theory after. — [jazzguitar.be](https://www.jazzguitar.be/forum/improvisation/60366-how-do-you-visualise-see-scales-arpeggios-during-progressions-2.html); [jazzguitar.be "no theory" thread](https://www.jazzguitar.be/forum/theory/83011-christiaan-van-hemert-once-again-championing-no-theory-12.html)
- *Technique as a goal.* Martin sells daily technique etudes and lists "Scales" among his 7 habits. — [Open Studio](https://www.openstudiojazz.com/?p=520955). Werner and Evans de-emphasize covering technical material in favor of depth and freedom from judgment. — [Berklee](https://www.berklee.edu/node/2474676); [Presentation Zen](https://presentationzen.com/blog/lessons-from-bill-evans)

### Inferences
Preliminary 80/20 ranking for a classically trained pianist, synthesized from the consensus above. This is my inference, not any one educator's list:
1. **Daily focused listening plus learning by ear from recordings**: melodies first, then short solo fragments (Terry, Aebersold, Martin, Iverson, Harris's path).
2. **Learn a small set of tunes deeply** (melody, changes, form, memorized, eventually in several keys), starting with blues (Aebersold, LJS, Iverson, Evans's "simple but real").
3. **Left-hand / two-hand voicing fluency as automatic drill in all keys**, chosen against the melody (Levine A/B positions, DeGreg). Classical technique makes this fast.
4. **Chord-tone/guide-tone lines landing on strong beats** via Galper target notes or Ligon outlines. This is the bridge between ear vocabulary and the changes, and it satisfies both camps.
5. **Swing time and upbeat-driven phrasing** (Galper/Gillespie). For classical players this is likely the largest gap, since classical training doesn't build swing feel.
6. **Sing lines; imitate articulation and feel** (Aebersold, Terry).
7. **Minimal theory as a map, not an engine**: enough chord-scale or Harris-6th-diminished knowledge to name what you hear. Both the pro-Aebersold and anti-scale camps agree this isn't the core of the sound (Iverson's concession).
- The contested scale debate matters less for a pianist who already reads and knows scales cold. Their risk is over-relying on scales precisely because they're easy for them, which is Iverson's "simulacrum" warning.

### Gaps
- No quantitative or empirical comparison of methods (e.g., Harris-system vs. chord-scale students) was found; all positions are expert opinion.
- Reddit r/jazzpiano threads didn't surface in search; forum consensus here comes from jazzguitar.be, TalkBass, and PianoGroove instead.
- "Metronome on 2 and 4" as a practice: no source was found in this session, though it is widely recommended in practice. To verify.

## Q3. What order or sequence do they recommend?

### Takeaway
The recurring sequence is: listen/imitate → blues → simple standards with ii-V-Is (Autumn Leaves) → rhythm changes. Within each tune: melody → changes/guide tones → voicings → improvising from chord tones. The philosophical sequence (Terry, Evans, Werner) is imitate before innovate and simple-real before advanced.

### Cited Findings
- Terry: imitation → assimilation → innovation. Students who skip to innovation struggle without a model. — [JazzAdvice](https://www.jazzadvice.com/lessons/clark-terrys-3-steps-to-learning-improvisation/)
- Evans: master something simple that's real before approximating something advanced. — [Presentation Zen](https://presentationzen.com/blog/lessons-from-bill-evans)
- Aebersold: start by improvising over one scale for confidence, then blues in Bb and F, then standard melodies such as "Satin Doll" and "Perdido." — [The Instrumentalist](https://theinstrumentalist.com/january-2018/after-50-years-of-play-a-longs-perspectives-on-jazz-education/)
- Practitioner tradition: 12-bar blues and variations first, then Autumn Leaves and/or rhythm changes. — [TalkBass thread](https://www.talkbass.com/threads/jazz-standards.1002447/) (forum opinion)
- Galper's practice order: target notes alone first, then single-note approaches from above, then pickups added to scales and arpeggios. — [JazzGuitarLessons.net](https://learn.jazzguitarlessons.net/blog/forward-motion); [halgalper.com](https://halgalper.com/articles/understandingforwardmotion/)
- Ligon's order: start with basic 3rd-to-7th resolutions, then the three outlines, then embellishments. — [JazzGuitarLessons.net](https://learn.jazzguitarlessons.net/blog/connecting-chords-with-linear-harmony)
- DeGreg's order: 2-voice → 7-voice structures, then 4ths and upper-structure triads. — [phildegreg.com](https://phildegreg.com/book/)
- Levine: left-hand voicings (A/B positions) early, then altering notes in those voicings, then two-handed voicings and comping. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jazz_Piano_Book)
- Harris 6th-diminished practice order: scale up, down, random → in 3rds → harmonized as chords. — [FreeJazzLessons](https://www.freejazzlessons.com/?p=13635)
- Werner: steps 1–3 (thoughtless touch, movement, playing what you know without judgment) come before step 4, studying new material. — [Berklee](https://www.berklee.edu/node/2474676)
- Learn Jazz Standards: memorize longer forms such as Autumn Leaves early. — [LJS](https://www.learnjazzstandards.com/blog/20-easy-jazz-standards/)

### Inferences
- Suggested sequence for this learner (inference): Week 1 onward, listen daily. First block: F/Bb blues (melodies like "Bag's Groove," "C-Jam Blues," "Blue Monk") with shell or Levine A/B left-hand voicings, guide-tone lines, blues-scale and chord-tone improvising, swing feel. Second block: Autumn Leaves and a minor blues ("Mr. P.C.") for major/minor ii-V-I, starting Ligon/Galper target-note work. Third block: rhythm changes ("Lester Leaps In") plus deeper voicings (DeGreg upper structures) and short solo transcriptions. Harris 6th-diminished harmony fits as an optional parallel track once voicings are fluent.
- Voicings vs. tunes isn't an either/or for a pianist. Levine's design (left-hand voicings chosen against the melody note) implies learning voicings inside tunes rather than in isolation.

### Gaps
- No primary source found where Levine, Harris, or Martin give an explicit week-by-week order for adult beginners.
- Open Studio's "Jazz Piano for Beginners" curriculum order wasn't accessible beyond the marketing description.

## Q4. Which small set of tunes do educators recommend first, and why?

### Takeaway
Blues in F and Bb is the most cited starting point, followed by Autumn Leaves as the archetypal first standard (major and minor ii-V-I around the circle of 4ths). Then Blue Bossa, a minor blues (Mr. P.C.), and a rhythm-changes head. Aebersold specifically names "Satin Doll" and "Perdido."

### Cited Findings
- Aebersold: "numerous blues in Bb and F," plus "Perdido" and "Satin Doll," as basic material everyone has to learn. — [The Instrumentalist](https://theinstrumentalist.com/january-2018/after-50-years-of-play-a-longs-perspectives-on-jazz-education/)
- Learn Jazz Standards' "20 Easy Jazz Standards" (order as published), with reasons:
  - **All Blues**: 6/4 blues with long dominant stretches; study the Miles solo.
  - **Autumn Leaves**: AABC on the circle of 4ths; teaches major and minor ii-V-I and voice leading; "one of the best tunes to memorize early."
  - **Blue Monk**: simple melody, syncopated rhythm, an intro to Monk.
  - **Blue Bossa**: 16-bar bossa with major and minor ii-V-Is and a key change.
  - **Bag's Groove**: blues for swing feel and phrasing.
  - **C-Jam Blues**: two-note melody.
  - **Cold Duck Time**: straight-8ths, I-IV.
  - **Doxy**: all-dominant tune in Bb.
  - **Fly Me to the Moon**: C major for most of the form.
  - **Mack the Knife**: four chords; half-step key rise each chorus.
  - **Lester Leaps In**: simplified rhythm changes.
  - **Mr. P.C.**: minor blues.
  - **My Little Suede Shoes**: ii-V-Is, an intro to Parker.
  - **Song for My Father**: modal F minor, V7sus.
  - **St. Thomas**: repetitive harmony; study the Rollins solo.
  - **Summertime**
  - **There Is No Greater Love**: ii-Vs, dominants.
  - **Watermelon Man**: straight-8ths.
  - **Work Song**: modal F minor.
  - **What Is This Thing Called Love**: minor ii-V to maj7.
  
  — [Learn Jazz Standards](https://www.learnjazzstandards.com/blog/20-easy-jazz-standards/)
- Autumn Leaves is the most consistently named first standard. It introduces fundamental progressions, and the solo can largely be handled with one parent scale, "that's why this is one of the first standards students learn." — [LJS podcast/blog](https://www.learnjazzstandards.com/?p=24038); [jazzguitar.be "best first jazz standard"](https://www.jazzguitar.be/forum/songs/4557-best-first-jazz-standard-learn-2.html)
- A teacher on Reddit introduces "Equinox" early and says "A Train" shouldn't come first for total beginners. — [r/jazzguitar via mirror](https://lr.ggtyler.dev/r/jazzguitar/comments/1fmbf2d/must_learn_jazz_standards) (forum)
- Satin Doll is used to teach swing rhythm and comping. — [Sylvia Brooks](https://sylviabrooks.net/jazz-standards-to-help-you-learn)
- Iverson's canon emphasis is blues, the American Songbook, and Monk. — [Iverson](https://ethaniverson.com/received-wisdom-jeff-goldblum-chord-scales-the-ireal-book-and-kamasi-washington/)

### Inferences
- Core starter set for this learner (inference, overlapping the most-cited lists): F blues (Bag's Groove / C-Jam / Blue Monk / Now's the Time) → Autumn Leaves → Blue Bossa → Mr. P.C. (minor blues) → Satin Doll → a rhythm-changes head (Lester Leaps In / Oleo) → All the Things You Are or There Is No Greater Love. This covers blues, major and minor ii-V-I, AABA forms, and rhythm changes, which together account for most of the standard repertoire's harmonic movement.
- The reason educators give is consistent: early tunes are chosen to isolate one harmonic device (dominant blues, ii-V-I, minor ii-V, modal vamp) with a simple melody that can be learned by ear.

### Gaps
- "Now's the Time," "Oleo," and "All the Things You Are" are common first-tune recommendations in background knowledge but weren't verified in a source this session.
- No primary list from Barry Harris's workshop repertoire was retrieved. To verify: workshop staples such as "Just One of Those Things" and Bud Powell/Parker tunes.

## Q5. What do they say are the biggest wastes of time or common traps?

### Takeaway
The traps named by primary sources are: playing scales or patterns detached from the melody, blues, and the sound of records; skipping imitation to "be original"; random notes with no relation to the harmony or the rhythm section; covering too much material shallowly; mechanical repetition without hearing; ego, perfectionism, and judgment; and treating "one" as the start of phrases rather than the destination.

### Cited Findings
- Scales disconnected from the language: "the sound of chord scales disconnected from bebop and the blues has inexorably crept into the fabric of common-practice jazz." — [Iverson](https://ethaniverson.com/received-wisdom-jeff-goldblum-chord-scales-the-ireal-book-and-kamasi-washington/)
- Relying on a chord table (iReal) instead of the melody and records. — [Iverson](https://ethaniverson.com/received-wisdom-jeff-goldblum-chord-scales-the-ireal-book-and-kamasi-washington/)
- Pattern-playing for its own sake: Harris reportedly disliked musicians who "just like sitting around playing patterns." — [Tune Into Leadership](https://www.tuneintoleadership.com/newsletter/barry-harris-keeper-of-the-bebop-flame) (secondhand)
- Skipping imitation: many students "want to skip to [innovation] as soon as they start learning how to improvise." — [JazzAdvice](https://www.jazzadvice.com/lessons/clark-terrys-3-steps-to-learning-improvisation/)
- Random playing: Aebersold criticizes those who "play seemingly randomly" and solo with no musical relationship to the rhythm section. — [The Instrumentalist](https://theinstrumentalist.com/january-2018/after-50-years-of-play-a-longs-perspectives-on-jazz-education/)
- Ego and fear of sounding bad: the ego "does not want you to sound bad." — [The Instrumentalist](https://theinstrumentalist.com/january-2018/after-50-years-of-play-a-longs-perspectives-on-jazz-education/). Werner: "When we try to play better, we play worse," and tying self-worth to performance and "Music School Disease" are damaging. — [Berklee](https://www.berklee.edu/node/2474676)
- Too much material: Werner says the ego pushes you to cover more material in less time, and you implement more when you cover less. — [Substack summary](https://guitarpractices.substack.com/p/effortless-mastery-part-3-doing-the). Evans: approximating something advanced you don't understand gives you nothing to build on. — [Presentation Zen](https://presentationzen.com/blog/lessons-from-bill-evans)
- Impatience: Evans says people quit because they can't conquer the problem immediately. — [Presentation Zen](https://presentationzen.com/blog/lessons-from-bill-evans)
- Mechanical repetition: Galper calls it outmoded and wants the ear retrained instead. Hearing "one" as a starting point stops the line. — [halgalper.com](https://halgalper.com/articles/understandingforwardmotion/)
- Unfocused long sessions: "You can do more in 10 minutes of focused practice than in an hour of sighing and moaning." Also "don't show off" and "practice what you can't play." — [Wynton Marsalis Foundation](https://wyntonmarsalis.org/news/entry/wynton-marsalis-12-tips-on-how-to-practice-for-musicians-athletes-anyone)
- Classical-background-specific: a PianoGroove member describes how the classical strength of precise note-for-note reproduction makes improvisation feel intimidating. A teacher responds that a classical base is a solid foundation for jazz piano. — [PianoGroove community](https://community.pianogroove.com/t/introduce-yourself-to-the-pianogroove-community/1216/67)
- Root-position "boxy" voicings with no voice leading are named as the amateur jazz-piano sound. — [The Maestro Online](https://www.the-maestro-online.com/blog/piano-improvisation-mistakes-solutions/) (lower-authority teaching blog)
- Transcribed material can take a long time to show up in your playing, so treat it as a "musical investment." — [MusicPlayer forum](https://forums.musicplayer.com/topic/180771-jazz-piano-listening-learning-or-both) (forum)

### Inferences
- The traps most specific to an advanced classical pianist (inference):
  - **Reading instead of hearing.** Learning tunes from lead sheets or iReal rather than records, which misses phrasing, melody, and feel (Iverson).
  - **Using technique to cover too much material.** Many voicings and scales learned shallowly (Werner, Evans).
  - **Even-eighth, downbeat-oriented phrasing** carried over from classical playing (Galper).
  - **Perfectionism and fear of wrong notes** (Werner, Aebersold's "ego").
  - **Practicing scales up and down as finger exercises**, which a classical player can do effortlessly but which doesn't generate jazz vocabulary (Iverson, Harris, Galper).
- Two sides of the same trap: Aebersold-style scale soloing gives quick confidence but risks the "simulacrum" sound, while pure transcription without application gives a slow return (the "investment" comment). A balanced plan pairs each transcribed fragment with immediate application on a tune in several keys, as in Terry's assimilate stage.
